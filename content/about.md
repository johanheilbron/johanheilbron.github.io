He is directeur de recherche émérite at the CNRS, attached to the Centre européen de sociologie et de science politique (CESSP) in Paris, and emeritus at Erasmus University Rotterdam, where he was professor of economic sociology and founding director of the Erasmus Center for Economic Sociology (2008–2018).

He studied philosophy and sociology at the University of Amsterdam and in Paris, and obtained his doctorate at the University of Amsterdam under Johan Goudsblom. In the early 1980s he worked at Pierre Bourdieu's Centre de sociologie européenne. He joined the CNRS in 1996 and held the Norbert Elias chair at Utrecht University and Erasmus University Rotterdam from 2000 to 2008.

His research covers three fields. The first is the historical sociology of the social and human sciences: the genesis of social theory, the formation of disciplines and national traditions, and the transnational history of the social sciences. The second is the sociology of translation and international cultural exchange. The third is economic sociology, from financial regimes and shareholder value to the pharmaceutical industry. His books include *The Rise of Social Theory* (1995) and *French Sociology* (2015), which received the Distinguished Publication Award of the American Sociological Association's History of Sociology Section.

He has been a fellow of the Wissenschaftskolleg zu Berlin, the Swedish Collegium for Advanced Study, NIAS and the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. He received the George Sarton Medal (2014) and an honorary doctorate from Uppsala University (2018), and is a member of the Academia Europaea.
